# Example Journey: From Signal To Decision

This fictional example shows the operating loop end to end.

It is intentionally synthetic. The point is the method.

## 1. Raw Signal

A founder notices repeated complaints about municipal permitting:

- small contractors do not know what is happening after submission;
- city portals differ by municipality;
- expediters spend time chasing comments, missing documents, and resubmissions.

Evidence maturity: raw signal.

Decision implication: do not build yet.

## 2. Structured Observation

The first framing is too broad:

```text
Permitting is broken.
```

That is not a useful startup wedge.

The tighter observation is:

```text
The resubmission loop after city comments creates repeated coordination work.
```

Evidence maturity: structured observation.

Decision implication: narrow the search area.

## 3. Pattern

The repeated workflow has a visible work object:

- city comment packet;
- missing-document list;
- owner map;
- resubmission package;
- applicant-facing status update.

This is more useful than a generic permit tracker because it points to a concrete job.

Evidence maturity: pattern-backed candidate.

Decision implication: create a problem candidate.

## 4. Problem Candidate

Problem candidate:

```text
Permit expediters and small contractors may lose time translating city comments into resubmission action plans.
```

This is still not validation.

It is only strong enough to justify a discovery experiment.

Evidence maturity: problem candidate.

Decision implication: experiment.

## 5. Experiment

Discovery plan:

1. Shadow one real resubmission workflow.
2. Interview three permit expediters.
3. Interview five small contractors.
4. Ask what currently happens after city comments arrive.
5. Ask what creates rework or missed items.

Evidence maturity target: interview-backed problem.

Decision implication: continue only if the workflow creates repeated time loss or missed-item risk.

## 6. Decision Rule

Continue if:

- expediters say the workflow saves at least 30 minutes per resubmission;
- contractors can identify a painful missed-item or delay pattern;
- the output improves the resubmission package.

Narrow if:

- value exists only for one permit type;
- one municipality creates most of the pain.

Park if:

- users find the output useful but not worth paying for.

Kill if:

- existing city portals or simple checklists already solve the problem.

## 7. Current Decision

```text
Decision: experiment, do not promote to venture thesis.
```

The system worked because it resisted premature conviction.

The research moved forward, but only one level.

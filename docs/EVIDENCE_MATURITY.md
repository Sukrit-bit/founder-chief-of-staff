# Evidence Maturity

## Purpose

Founder research fails when every signal feels equally important.

This ladder keeps evidence honest.

## Ladder

| Level | Label | Meaning | Allowed Output |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Raw signal | A source, quote, company, conversation, or hunch. | Source note, open question. |
| 1 | Structured observation | The signal is organized and reusable. | Case study, source note, theme note. |
| 2 | Pattern-backed | Similar signal appears across multiple artifacts. | Pattern-register entry. |
| 3 | Problem candidate | User, workflow, pain, and workaround are visible. | Problem backlog item. |
| 4 | Interview-backed | Multiple users confirm pain, frequency, urgency, or spend. | Problem statement candidate. |
| 5 | Experiment-backed | Prototype, concierge workflow, or service test improves the job. | Promoted problem statement. |
| 6 | Venture-thesis candidate | Buyer, wedge, AI efficacy, expansion path, and founder fit are plausible. | Venture thesis draft. |
| 7 | Decision-ready | Riskiest assumptions are tested enough for a strategic call. | Decision log entry. |

## Anti-Inflation Rule

Public momentum is not customer validation.

Funding announcements, accelerator batches, social posts, and competitor scans can support patterns. They do not prove willingness to pay.
